A biblical reflection

Staying Within the Teaching of Christ

A call to hold fast to the gospel, test every teaching, and keep Christ at the centre.

By Dr. Harris Andoh2 John 1:9–11 · GNT

Across Africa today, the church is growing at a remarkable pace—and that is something to thank God for. Alongside this growth, however, there is a worrying trend: new teachings, prophetic “revelations,” spiritual formulas, and rituals are sometimes added to what Christ taught.

Some churches promote anointing oil, holy water, “miracle” handkerchiefs, or payment for blessings as ways to receive God’s favour. Others place visions, dreams, and the sayings of charismatic leaders on the same level as Scripture, or even above it. Still others blend Christianity with traditional customs and ancestral practices. This is not limited to Africa. Around the world, human traditions, philosophies, and personal experiences can be given authority equal to the Word of Christ.

The apostle John addressed this danger directly:

“Anyone who does not stay with the teaching of Christ, but goes beyond it, does not have God. Whoever does stay with the teaching has both the Father and the Son.”

2 John 1:9, GNT

The dangers of going beyond the Scriptures

1. It separates a person from God

John is blunt: whoever goes beyond Christ’s teaching “does not have God.” This is not about minor differences of opinion. To add to Christ’s teaching is to move away from the very relationship that gives life. Fellowship with the Father and the Son is found by remaining in what Christ taught.

2. It suggests that Christ’s teaching is incomplete

Anyone who adds to the words of Christ is, in effect, saying that what He gave us was not enough. Yet Jesus declared that He made known everything He received from the Father (John 15:15). Scripture warns that nothing should be added to God’s word (Deuteronomy 4:2; Revelation 22:18). To go beyond it is to place human wisdom above divine wisdom.

3. It opens the door to deception

When the Word of God is no longer the final standard, there is nothing by which to test teachers and teachings. Experiences, emotions, and strong personalities can become the measure of truth. Paul warned that some would turn away from the truth and follow teachers who say what people want to hear (2 Timothy 4:3–4). Without Scripture as our anchor, believers can be “tossed about by every wind of teaching” (Ephesians 4:14).

4. It leads to the exploitation of believers

Many false teachings come with a price tag. Where leaders claim special revelation beyond Christ, ordinary believers can be pressured into paying for blessings, depending on a “man of God” for access to God, and living in fear. Christ, however, invites all to come to Him freely (Matthew 11:28).

5. It divides the church and weakens its witness

Teachings that go beyond Christ create factions, rivalries, and confusion. Instead of presenting one Lord, one faith, and one baptism (Ephesians 4:5), the church can become a collection of competing voices, leaving the watching world wondering what the gospel really is.

6. It requires believers to be vigilant

John makes clear that this is not a matter to be treated casually. Verse 10 instructs believers not to welcome those who come without the true teaching of Christ, and not even to greet them with “Peace be with you.” This is a warning against endorsing or helping to spread teaching that distorts the gospel—not a licence for hostility toward people.

Every believer has a responsibility to test what is taught against the Word, as the Bereans did when they examined the Scriptures daily to see whether Paul’s message was true (Acts 17:11). Vigilance means being discerning, grounded in Scripture, and faithful to Christ.

Hold fast to Christ

John closes this passage with a sober warning: “For anyone who wishes them peace becomes their partner in the evil things they do” (2 John 1:11, GNT). Welcoming and endorsing teachers who go beyond the teaching of Christ is not a neutral act of kindness. It makes us participants in the damage their teaching does.

Let every believer and every church therefore hold firmly to the words of Christ, test every teaching by the Scriptures, and refuse to give a platform to anything that adds to or departs from what He has taught—so that we may truly have both the Father and the Son.

A moment to reflect

What teaching or spiritual practice have you encountered recently? Take time to examine it in Scripture, pray for wisdom, and keep the teaching of Christ as your sure foundation.

HA

Dr. Harris AndohBaptist International Worship Centre, Abelenkpe, Accra
